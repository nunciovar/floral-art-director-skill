# Test 05 — Luxury Style

## Test Objective

Verify that an opulent, high-end reference is translated into **quality and restraint**, not cheap decorative clutter. Luxury in a bouquet comes from rare-but-real material, craftsmanship, and focused accents — never from piling on trinkets.

## Input Scenario

A luxury reference image: dark velvet tones (deep burgundy, aubergine, near-black), a single gold highlight, champagne and cream light, rich texture. The user asks for a "high-end, luxurious bouquet" in this mood, for a formal occasion.

No budget, size, or recipient is given (defaults: medium budget — but a *luxury-style* design may justify higher-end material; the spend must still be expressed as floristry, not decoration).

## Expected Behavior

- The dark palette is carried by **real, naturally dark material**: burgundy garden roses, dark dahlias, chocolate cosmos, black hellebore, deep purple calla lilies, dark foliage (black knight buddleia, dark pittosporum, smoke bush).
- The single **gold highlight** from the reference is used as a focused focal or accent — one restrained element (e.g. a gold-tinged rose or a thin gilded wire accent), not a wash of metallic decoration.
- Champagne/cream tones provide the transition and lift.
- Quality cues come from **material choice and craftsmanship**: lush texture, precise wrapping in matte or textured papers, satin or velvet ribbon — not from decorative quantity.
- Any accessory (pearl spray, crystal, feather) is **strictly limited to one or two supporting elements** that serve the concept.
- The BOM stays coherent with exact quantities and roles.

## Failure Mode

- ❌ **Cheap decoration overload** — glitter, pearls, ribbons, feathers, and acrylic all added at once because the prompt said "luxury".
- ❌ Confusing luxury with *quantity* of ornaments rather than quality of flowers and finish.
- ❌ Using artificially colored or obviously dyed material to fake the dark or gold tones without labeling it.
- ❌ Losing floral hierarchy — too many focal points competing for attention.
