# Test 04 — Minimal Style

## Test Objective

Verify that a quiet, sparse, minimal reference does **not** get translated into a dense, over-stuffed bouquet. The design must respect negative space and restraint, and the BOM must stay small and disciplined.

## Input Scenario

A minimal reference image: a single pale stem against a large expanse of white or soft grey, a thin horizon line, muted tones, huge negative space. The user asks for a bouquet "like this — quiet, minimal."

No budget, size, or recipient is given (defaults: medium budget, medium-size gift bouquet — but minimalism takes precedence over default size/density).

## Expected Behavior

- The design preserves **negative space** as a first-class element: open, asymmetrical structure, few elements, visible stems.
- The BOM is deliberately **small** — a handful of focal/lift pieces, one or two foliage types, minimal or no filler. Not a trophy bouquet.
- Filler material is omitted or used only where the reference actually has texture.
- Line and structure carry the design (thin line flowers, single strong focal, clean stems, simple wrap) rather than mass and volume.
- The wrapping and auxiliaries are simple and monochrome-consistent with the palette.
- The render brief shows sparse spacing, not a full dense head.

## Failure Mode

- ❌ **Over-stuffing** — translating "minimal" into a full, dense bouquet with 10+ flower types.
- ❌ Adding default medium-budget mass (roses, carnations, heavy filler) that the minimal reference never implied.
- ❌ Decorating with ribbons, butterflies, or accents that break the quiet.
- ❌ A render brief that depicts dense mass while the concept claims minimalism — concept/render inconsistency.
